# Storyboard

Outputs: `episodes/<ep>/storyboard.json` (build input; canonical unless segments exist), optional `segments/*.json`, and the generated `storyboard.md`. Visual rules come from `brief.md` (medium per mechanism, props table, negative list) and the art direction it names: read only that file in `.claude/skills/art-direction/references/directions/` before planning. Its motion and text sections override the house values here; the limits marked *channel* do not move for any direction.

## 1. Order of work
Start only from a passed script and a locked brief: a brief marked "look not locked" goes back to `episode-brief` §3 for its proof frames first.
1. **Voice first, or word anchors.** After script-review passes, `voice-narration` extracts lines straight from `script.md` and synthesizes them, so durations are real before any shot is planned. A storyboard may be drafted beside the voice only when every action is keyed to a word (`word#n`), never to seconds, and resolved from `voice/words` at build; ep03 did this on the lead's instruction and lost nothing.
2. **Shots carry meaning.** For each script scene, list what the viewer must see change. Each beat (one narration paragraph) must change the active element: a reveal, highlight, move, count, camera stop or new node. Decorative motion (grain, pulsing, drift) is not a change. *Channel:* **nothing on screen is still for 4 s or longer, and no hold goes over 8 s.** A *still* is a stretch where nothing moves; a *hold* is a stretch with no new change of meaning (a reveal, a count, a camera stop). Ep01's 1–2 reveals across 3–5 beats produced 10–23 s holds (S01 19 s, the S07 avalanche 21 s, the S16 NIST run 45 s, the S18 recap 32 s). **Rests and the direction's "no idle animation":** a planned rest (breath ~1.6 s, scene tail ~2.2 s, a clipping hold ~2.5 s) stays fully still, as the operator asked, for up to 4 s; after that the next change of meaning comes or the shot cuts. This is a cap on stillness, not the withdrawn motion quota: nothing is added to keep a frame busy, rests stay still, they just end. Two exceptions: an evidence window (a clipping or document whose marker lands on its phrase) may hold up to its existing 12 s limit, and the end tail plus end card are outside the 4 s cap. Ep03's QA failures (9.7 s and 8.3 s) were rests and no-op cues that ran past both limits.
   **Mechanism big and isolated** (*channel*). The beat's mechanism is the largest thing in frame, shown on the part that matters (the changed bits, the matching drawer, the one field that differs), with everything else dimmed or out of frame, readable at 360 px wide. A whole machine with the important part at 40 px is the ep03 pilot's stamp again.
   **Big gaps look big** (*channel*). A headline contrast is drawn on a scale that feels as large as it is: time the viewer can feel (seventeen seconds on a stopwatch against twelve years of calendar pages), a tiny object beside a huge one, a bar that runs off the frame. No log-scale bars for a headline contrast; ep01's 164 billion against 7 thousand on log bars read as a modest difference (`LEARNINGS.md`, Episode 2 storyboard rules 3).
   **A document proves; it does not explain** (*channel*). In the direction's evidence treatment, a run is page → highlighted passage → back to the object or flow that the claim changes: at most two beats or 12 s of documents in a row, and never a whole scene on one page. A changing highlight box on the same blurred page counts as the same picture; ep01 held one NIST page for 45 s across four rules. Show a list of rules as what happens to the running example in the flow, with the page as the proof.
   **Never a slide deck** (*channel*; operator, 2026-11-13: "I meant dont make a ppt. the video has same bg but action keeps on happening in a flow"). One background may hold for the whole act; on it each action leads into the next, built piece by piece, never a static picture swapped for another static picture. A change of colour field or a visible cut inside an act needs its reason written in the storyboard row (a state change in the direction's colour script is one). Ep02 changed the frame about every 5 s with 36 hard cuts (`research/reference-Jktv8v8ICpk.md` (not in repo) §8). A breath rest, scene tail or clipping hold is a pause inside the flow, not a slide; this is not a motion quota, and "something must move every N s" stays withdrawn.
   **The character**, only in a format that uses one (`script-writing` §2). Whoever the story follows is on screen from the first beat in the direction's character treatment. Their words appear beside the face, never on it, and the part of the frame holding the character stays fixed (`research/study-youtube-video-creation.md` (not in repo) §5).
3. **Pick the visual per shot** from the component library first (`video/src/components/LIBRARY.md`, `art-direction` §5), then the registry (`video/src/registry.ts`), following the brief's medium table and the components the direction file's §10 names. A new component is built only when no library entry fits; the row says which entries were checked. Visual missing → build it in `video-build` with a gallery entry first; never put a placeholder name in the storyboard.
4. **Truth pass.** Every number, hash, name or quote in `props` maps to a VERIFIED fact or a recorded computation, and reads identically in every shot that shows it (ep01's recap printed a ciphertext from an older key). Then walk each scene line by line: every object at rest is a claim about the line being spoken, and a state changes on the word that changes it. Ep03 QA caught what stills review missed: a code step left on the phone under "Your phone checks", `Passkey ON` a second after "Make one", `password · deleted` while the narration said the app won't let you, and `mango@123` gone before "one two three" (a value the narrator spells out stays whole until its last spoken part). A hypothetical shown on one app's own screen binds every later beat of that screen.
5. **Timed proof** before any full render: `bun tools/build-episode.ts episodes/<ep> <id>`, then `(cd video && node stills.mjs <id> /tmp/stills-<id>)` (one frame at 70 % of each shot; while Boat is up, take the same frames with `tools/boat/still.sh` (not in repo) instead, `video-build` §1); review at 360 px wide, then run `video-build` §6 lead review on them and send them to the operator as a review page (§6), stills before render (`video-build` §1).
6. **Pilot.** `video-build` builds the first act (about the first 60–90 s) at finished quality and the operator watches it before any other act is built; their notes change the storyboard, not only act 1 (`research/study-youtube-video-creation.md` (not in repo) §3). Ask of the pilot: what is the hero object, and is it the biggest readable thing in frame? Ep03's pilot showed its stamp as a ~40 px square.

## 2. storyboard.json (as `tools/build-episode.ts` reads it)
```json
{"fps": 30, "width": 1920, "height": 1080, "voice": "<season narrator voice id>", "musicVolume": 1,
 "scenes": [{"id": "S02", "act": 1, "title": null, "transitionIn": "cut",
   "shots": [{"visual": "DocShot",
     "props": {"page": "rockyou.png", "quote": "rockyou-quote.png", "quoteStep": 1, "source": "TechCrunch, 14 Dec 2009"},
     "beats": [{"id": "S02_01", "vo": "In December two thousand nine, a company called Rock You was hacked."}],
     "sfx": ["hit"]}]}]}
```
- `transitionIn` ∈ `cut | fade | push | zoom | wipe` (`video/src/types.ts`). House use, unless the direction's motion section says otherwise: `cut` inside an act only between scenes on the same stage, so the cut does not show (a visible one carries its reason, §1.2), `fade` at an act start; others only when the motion means something (a `zoom` into a mechanism). No action starts under a transition (ep03 v1 opened a door during a fade).
- Rests: `pause` on a beat (seconds after it; the scene's last beat takes the scene or act pause instead), `pauseScene` / `pauseAct` at the top level, replace the defaults in `video-build` §2. Set them to the direction's rests.
- `title` (non-null) starts a chapter in `out/chapters.txt`; set it on the first scene of each chapter, in the viewer's words (ep02 shipped "Callback and exit"), and put the part's count or name on screen as it starts (a row label, "3 / 4"). `package-review` checks the names.
- Beat ids and `vo` must equal `voice/lines.json`; `python3 tools/sync-storyboard-vo.py episodes/<ep>` copies narration from the script and fails when a scene's beat count changed.
- `python3 tools/beat-frames.py episodes/<ep> <beat ids…>` estimates beat start frames inside a shot before the build (same rounding as the builder); after `build-episode.ts` the exact offsets are the shot's `steps` in `video/src/episodes/<id>.json`, and components receive those. Animate on beat starts. Shots ending a scene or act run longer and the episode's last shot has a 2.5 s tail: hold the last pose.
- If the script's ten-rule notes adopt a takeaway end card (`script-writing` §2), the last shot ends on it at card-text size (§4), held through the tail so it can be read and screenshot.
- A clipping lands on the word that names its source, the marker on the next phrase (ep02's Microsoft clipping sat unmarked 9.7 s; on the name, 2.4 s). A value that has been used leaves before anything else lands on its place (ep02's "418093" stayed under the guess slips).
- **Sound on every visual action**: each move, reveal, count, lift and camera stop gets its sound, as Kurzgesagt and Fireship do. The old cap ("at most one per 10 s") is removed; ep01 had about 7 effects in 10 minutes. Which sound, its level against voice and music, and the episode's sound plan follow `.claude/skills/sound-design/SKILL.md`; the cue list lives in the episode's sound plan, and shot `sfx` lists stay empty (the sound is mixed as one stem; `sound-design` §5).

## 3. Segments (parallel builds)
Split by act into `segments/<X>.json`, each a **scene array** (not the whole object); `storyboard.json` then supplies only the top-level fields. Order: edit segment → `python3 tools/merge-segments.py episodes/<ep>` (checks every narration line appears once, in order, verbatim) → `build-episode.ts`. Edits made directly to a merged `storyboard.json` are overwritten. Narration changes follow `voice-narration` §6 (segments branch).

## 4. Text on screen
| Role | Minimum at 1080p, measured in the render as the height of a capital letter |
|---|---|
| Anything the viewer must read: labels, values, claim-bearing UI, a lifted value, a highlighted crop | **30 px** (about a 42 px font in Inter) |
| Call to action, end card, headline, the number the narration speaks | 44 px, ≤ 7 words |
| Background UI or document detail the viewer need not read | any size, if nothing the narration claims sits in it |

**Why 30 px everywhere** (*channel*; resolves `LEARNINGS.md`'s "36 px text floor" against this table's old 20 px): one floor, measured the way a QA check reads a frame, replaces two numbers that disagreed. 20 px was too low: ep03's phone UI at 19 to 27 px was about 5 px tall at 360 px wide, and the independent visual review scored legibility 2 of 5. The 36 px floor was a font size, not a measured letter, and clipping quotes at 34 to 36 px still failed at phone width (`LEARNINGS.md`, ep03 QA). A 30 px capital is about 10 px tall on a 360 px phone, the smallest that reads in one glance. A direction's §7 may set more, never less.

Everything inside 90 % title-safe and out of the bottom 15 % (captions and mobile controls). A label that only reads at 360 px wide below these sizes means enlarge the prop or move the camera, not shrink the rule.

**Words that must be read** pass the size rule and can still fail on a phone: ep01's thin condensed serif at ~40 px, pale on a pale backdrop or red on brown and teal, was unreadable at 360 px wide. *Channel:* contrast ≥ 4.5:1 between the ink and what is directly behind it (fix it with a plate, scrim or surface under the text rather than by recolouring), a weight that survives 360 px, and, when the text is the number the narration speaks, the largest text in the frame. Whether words may float over a scene at all, and in which face, is the direction's §7.

## 5. Before build
- Every beat has a visual change; nothing still for 4 s or more; no hold > 8 s; headline contrasts on a scale that feels as large as they are; no more than 12 s of documents in a row. Measure holds from the cue list, not the stills: a cue that changes nothing visible at 360 px is part of the hold around it (ep03 v3's "fingerprint" and "number" cues were no-ops and became QA r3's 9.7 s and 8.3 s blockers).
- No slide deck: every in-act field change or visible cut has its written reason; where the format uses a character, it is in frame from the first beat; chapter names in the viewer's words; the end card, if adopted.
- Every word the viewer must read is ≥ 30 px letter height at 1080p, was read in a 360 px still and has ≥ 4.5:1 contrast.
- Every visual action has a sound cue (`sound-design`).
- Every factual prop passed the truth pass; script changes were propagated into props (sync only copies narration).
- (After the timed build) `python3 tools/storyboard-md.py episodes/<ep> video/src/episodes/<id>.json` regenerated `storyboard.md` for review.

## 6. Operator review page
Stills, look options and pilot notes go to the operator as **one local HTML review page**, not Slack contact sheets and free-text replies. One card per shot, in film order: the settled still (large enough to judge at a glance, click to enlarge), the voice line it sits under, the shot id and time, and a comment box. At the top, the items that need the operator's answer; at the bottom, a box for anything else and one **Copy all comments** button that copies every non-empty comment with its shot id, ready to paste back into the session. Look options use the same page with one card per option. The page is a single self-contained file in `episodes/<ep>/reviews/`, opened locally; their pasted comments change the storyboard, and the page is rebuilt for the next round.

## Aim for (next to the NEVER list)
Frames to aim at, not only bans (`art-direction`, Aim for): a 3Blue1Brown-style mechanism shot (one mechanism isolated and enlarged, the changing part lit, the rest dimmed); Kurzgesagt scale (a tiny thing beside a huge one, a sound on every move); Fireship density (real code and screens at readable size, something new every few seconds); ByteByteGo flow (a request travelling through parts built piece by piece, drawn as real screens in screen-first, not labelled boxes).

## NEVER
- Never use a text card standing in for a mechanism, a decorative object, or a treatment in the direction's §9 (see `episode-brief`). In every direction, the same goes for a table or list of rows, a stat card, a chapter title card, a chart, boxes joined by arrows, and a frame of text alone (ep01 v1 "text, not visuals", v2 "a slide deck with doodles"; operator on ep03, 2026-11-15: "our episode 1 v1 had a similar style of tables that i disliked"). Mute test: with the narration muted, something happens to an object.
- Never draw real hardware from imagination when real footage or photos exist; the operator preferred real GPU footage. Treat it per the direction's §8.
- Never put image-generated text or numbers on screen; build them in code.
