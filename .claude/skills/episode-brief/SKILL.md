# Episode brief

Output: `episodes/<ep>/brief.md`. Structure mirrors a design brief (see `docs/research/Video creative process and skills.md`):

1. **Context** — the question, the viewer (smart non-expert), the promise of the title/thumbnail, target length.
2. **Packaging first** — 3 title options, 2 thumbnail concepts (≤4 words on image, one focal object, adds an angle the title doesn't state). Decide before the script.
3. **Source material** — the fact sheet and any real artefacts (standards pages, code, data) to show on screen.
4. **House style** — the channel style bible (palette, type, grid, grain, motion curves). Change it only deliberately.
5. **References** — numbered; for each: what to take, what to ignore. Synthesise shared principles; never copy one reference.
6. **What did not work** — the negative list (also used as negative prompts).
7. **Material** — texture comes from the process (grain, paper, CRT phosphor), never a filter laid on top.
8. **Motion language** — named easings and durations; how scenes enter/exit; transition vocabulary.
9. **Shot types** — the applications: cold open, title card, diagram build, data reveal, code, comparison, recap, end card, thumbnail, 9:16 cut.
10. **Colour script** — one line per act: dominant colour and what it means.
11. **Sound** — voice, pace (150–165 wpm), music bed level (−20 to −24 dB under VO), SFX palette.
12. **Expected output and checks** — which rubric applies (`video-qa`).
